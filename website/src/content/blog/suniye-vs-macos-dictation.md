## What macOS's Dictation actually does

macOS has had two dictation modes for years: Standard, which sends audio to Apple's servers and needs an internet connection, and Enhanced (called on-device dictation in newer macOS), which processes everything locally and needs none. On Apple Silicon Macs the on-device mode is genuinely private and needs no setup beyond turning it on. It's also fixed: one model, chosen by Apple, with no option to swap it for something faster or more accurate for your use case, and no cleanup pass — what you say is what lands, filler words and all.

## What Suniye adds

**Model choice.** Suniye ships eleven local speech models, from a 118 MB one that starts instantly to a 1.7 GB one built for accuracy — and Apple's own engine is one of the eleven, so switching to it costs you nothing you'd have kept by staying with the system default. You're not choosing Suniye *instead of* Apple's dictation; you can choose it *as* one of your options.

**Cleanup, optionally.** Magic Format turns a rambling take into clean text — fixing grammar, formatting lists, following a correction instead of transcribing it literally. It runs through Apple Intelligence or a local model by default, so it stays on-device the same way the transcription does. macOS's own dictation has no equivalent; what you say is exactly what appears.

**One shortcut, everywhere, that you control.** Suniye's hotkey works the same way across every app, and you pick it — not limited to whatever key macOS assigns to dictation in System Settings. Hold, speak, release.

**A history you can actually see.** Suniye keeps a local log of what you've dictated, with copy and delete controls in a dashboard. macOS's dictation keeps nothing — each dictation exists only in whatever field you typed it into.

**Per-app tone.** Give Slack a terse prompt and Mail a fuller one, and Suniye applies the right one automatically based on where you're typing. macOS's dictation has no concept of this — it behaves identically everywhere.

## When the built-in tool is genuinely enough

If you dictate occasionally, don't need cleanup, and are fine with whatever model Apple ships, macOS's on-device Dictation is free, already installed, and private on Apple Silicon. There's no dishonest answer here — for light, occasional use, it does the job with zero setup.

## When Suniye is worth installing

If you dictate often enough that model choice or a cleanup pass would save real time, or you want a history you can search back through, or a shortcut that isn't tied to System Settings — that's the gap Suniye fills, on top of the same on-device foundation macOS already gives you for free.
